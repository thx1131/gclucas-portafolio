The shift toward the digital age and automation has transformed our relationship with time, work, matter, and others. As we advance, certain things, habits, and ways of life are fading away. What is lost in the digital age is not merely a set of obsolete practices, but the very texture of human experience: its materiality and its difficulty. Where the system predicts, the body improvises. This piece explores the tension between algorithmic efficiency and the slowness of the human gesture. We run the risk of becoming solitary. Automating the world may mean, without our realizing it, uninhabiting it. The digital age and automation are not simply technical advances; they are profound mutations in our relationship with the world and with others. This is not only about what disappears, but about what kind of humanity is being shaped by those disappearances.
