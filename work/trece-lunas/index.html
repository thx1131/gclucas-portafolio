trece lunas

1998

Freedom to concentrate and freedom to disperse. Freedom to demolish the prisons of pictorial method and freedom to turn forms into residences of the imagination. Lucas sets out to bring these two powers together, and in part of his work represents them as the opposition between the geometric landscape (rigorous by vocation of line) and the landscape of beings and situations. In different paintings the opposites contend until they reach that supreme point which is integration. On examining the proposal, one might think that the realm most resistant to any modification — geometry, in principle — is the most institutional thing there is. But then again, is not the most conceivable institution actually chaos itself? And does not, for instance, the confrontation between a person and the harmonies of rhombuses and squares force us to recognize the indissoluble bond between order and disorder?

— Carlos Monsiváis
